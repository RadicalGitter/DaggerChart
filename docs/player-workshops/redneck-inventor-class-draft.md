# Inventor — Redneck's homebrew class

**Playtest proposal — GM-authorized offer, awaiting Amme's confirmation**  
Version: 0.1 — October 13, 2026  
Authors: Amme's character ideas, Oscar's workshop decisions, and assistant drafting  
Scope: a complete tabletop class proposal, including two subclasses and advancement through level 10. Oscar authorized a character-specific offer on October 13, 2026. It is excluded from the general creator; Amme's explicit confirmation applies it to Redneck's sheet.

The [Swedish player handout](redneck-inventor-class-sv.md) and in-app guide use
the same bilingual rules. The [reusable class-choice contract](../character-class-choices.md)
owns the offer, confirmation and sheet migration. Balance remains provisional.

The [workshop brief](amme-inventor-class-brief.md) records the character and
the workshop's boundaries. This document owns the proposed rules. The current
[settlement design](../settlement-design-spec.md) and [inventory contract](../inventory.md)
continue to govern the application and campaign data.

## The fantasy

An Inventor sees useful possibilities in the things everyone carries and the
materials around them. They build defenses, rig routes through danger, repair
equipment, and discover what unfamiliar materials can do. Their reliable work
comes from practice and tested knowledge. Their discoveries can become new
inventions worth taking home. Redneck's mechanical wings and folding stilts
belong to the Rigger path; other Inventors might travel with winches, ramps,
sleds, or portable bridges.

### Design goals

1. Let the player describe surprising inventions while the rules give their effects clear costs and limits.
2. Support travel and collective survival in every Inventor, with a stronger movement specialty for Riggers.
3. Make exploration produce useful material knowledge and practical objects, without tracking dozens of component currencies.

### Deliberate weaknesses

- Prepared Parts are a small session reserve. An Inventor must choose between helping rolls, restoring confidence, and building defenses.
- A defense protects a position and direction. Moving enemies, cramped spaces, and attacks that do not depend on that line of sight can change its usefulness.

Ordinary attacks, domain cards, tools, gathering, and fabrication remain useful
when Parts run out. Reliable equipment does not randomly malfunction just
because the character is an Inventor.

### Settled direction and draft choices

The workshop established Prepared Parts, a proactive defense costing one Part
for cover or two for blocked line of sight, gathering influenced by material
familiarity, and custom mundane items. Oscar's existing ruling permits
Redneck's agreed main trait.

This draft proposes the exact reserve rules, defense footprint and duration,
Hope Feature, domain pair, gathering procedure, and both subclass progressions.
These numbers need table playtesting. The written examples below are rules
walkthroughs, not evidence that the class is balanced.

## Class chassis

| Element | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| Class | **Inventor** |
| Domains | **Splendor + Valor** |
| Starting Evasion | **9** |
| Starting Hit Points | **7** |
| Starting Stress / Hope | The ordinary Daggerheart starting slots |
| Inventor trait | Choose a character trait at character creation to represent your method. This is also your **Spellcast trait** for either subclass. |
| Redneck's trait | Use the trait Oscar and Amme already agreed upon. This proposal does not redistribute his traits. |

Splendor supports field medicine and keeping companions alive. Valor supports
protection and forceful intervention. Keeping this pair preserves access to
Redneck's existing **Mending Touch** and **Forceful Push**. The new class
features supply construction, exploration, and battlefield preparation.

Domain cards retain their printed rules, costs, card types, and damage types.
The player can describe them as inventions or fantasy engineering; that
description does not create a permanent mundane item with the card's powers.
The class's gathering and ordinary fabrication features produce mundane items.

### Starting equipment

Use normal level-1 weapon and armor selection. Add an inventor's toolkit:
basic hand tools, reusable fasteners, and pieces for a small portable travel
rig. These explain the features below; they grant no additional numerical
weapon or armor bonuses.

Take the usual torch, 50 feet of rope, basic supplies, handful of gold, and
choice of Minor Health Potion or Minor Stamina Potion. Choose one personal item:

- A battered tool passed down by your family.
- A notebook with room for the discoveries you will bring home.

Redneck can retain his existing equipment. A Scepter can look like his own
constructed apparatus while keeping its existing statistics.

## Hope Feature: Pull Together

**When you have the spotlight, spend 3 Hope** to deploy a travel rig and move
yourself and up to **your Proficiency** willing allies within **Very Close
range** of you to a point within **Close range** of you. Describe how the rig
moves the group: a haul line, folding steps, a rolling platform, or another
practical method. Each participant may decline the movement.

The route must be physically possible for the described device. This feature
does not grant flight, pass through solid obstacles, or clear conditions that
prevent movement. An unavoidable environmental hazard still calls for its
ordinary roll. No Prepared Parts are required.

At level 1, this moves Redneck and one companion. As Proficiency rises, he can
bring more companions. It is a coordinated departure, not an interrupt after
an enemy's attack has already resolved.

## Class Feature: Prepared Parts

At the beginning of each session, roll a number of **d4s equal to your
Inventor trait**, with a minimum of one. Keep those dice and their results as
your Prepared Parts. Clear the previous reserve before rolling the new one.
Discard unused Parts when the session ends.

A **Part is one retained die**, not a point on its face. Each die is spent
once for one of these uses:

| Use | Cost and effect |
|---|---|
| **Improvise** | Spend any number of Parts to add their values to an action or reaction roll made by you or an ally within **Far range**. Describe the device, adjustment, or practical assistance. Spend after the roll is made but before its consequences resolve. The addition changes the total, not which Duality Die is higher. |
| **Restore Confidence** | Spend any number of Parts to give yourself or an ally within **Far range** Hope equal to their values, within the recipient's normal Hope limit. Describe a useful adjustment or dependable invention. |
| **Prepared Defense** | Spend **one Part** for cover, or **two Parts** for a defense that blocks line of sight. Their rolled values do not change the defense. Use the deployment rules below. |

The bonus can accompany ordinary Daggerheart assistance under the normal
stacking rules. The same Part cannot both increase a roll and grant Hope, or
also pay for a defense.

Gathering materials, packing up a defense, repairing equipment, and taking
rests do not refill this reserve. Ordinary materials do not convert into Parts.
This prevents a gathering-to-Hope loop.

### Prepared Defense: deployment

When you have the spotlight, spend the required Parts to assemble and deploy
a **small stationary defense** at a point within **Close range**. Use your
toolkit, carried gear, and suitable available materials. Describe what you
build and the direction it protects. You may reposition within Close range as
part of deploying it, following a physically possible route. The GM can call
for a roll for a hazardous route; ordinary assembly itself needs no extra roll.

Deployment is your substantive contribution to this spotlight. It does not
include a separate attack. It cannot be inserted into an attack already being
resolved. Your equipment must be able to reach the placement point; this
feature does not assemble an object remotely through an obstruction.

The base defense accommodates **one creature approximately your size or
smaller**, standing immediately behind it. It protects from **one chosen
direction** and lasts for the **current scene**, unless dismantled or destroyed.
It is a screen or compact barricade, not a room-sized wall. Close is the
placement range, not the protected area's radius.

- **One Part:** partial obstruction gives cover. An attack passing through it has disadvantage.
- **Two Parts:** total obstruction blocks line of sight through it. A ranged attack requiring sight cannot be made through it.

The barrier also obstructs attacks coming from its protected side. A creature
can change position to see or attack around it, losing its protection from
that direction. Cover gives no automatic damage reduction or Armor Slots.

An adversary that can physically reach and break or dismantle the defense may
use its spotlight to do so. That destruction does not itself also attack a
protected creature. An adversary can instead reposition around it when the
fiction allows. The GM describes how the counter works.

After the scene, the temporary assembly is packed up, exhausted, or no longer
holds together, as fits the construction. Ordinary borrowed gear can be
recovered; spent Parts remain spent. A permanent fortification is a separate
mundane construction or project. There is no roll-based refund.

Gear or materials belonging to another PC may be used only with that player's
agreement. Redneck cannot silently consume Kaja's equipment or decide what
happens to her hair.

## Class Feature: Scavenge and Build

Choose **two familiar material types** at character creation. Name materials
your character has actually worked with, such as a particular local timber,
hide, or stone. Broad entries such as "all wood" or "all metal" do not make
every new species or alloy familiar. Prepared equipment can use materials
your character already understands.

### Gather a batch

When you have access to a useful source and time to work, describe the
material or component you want. The GM establishes whether it is present,
what harvesting it requires, and any risk. If success is uncertain, make an
action roll using an appropriate trait or the agreed trait ruling. Add a
relevant Experience under the ordinary rules when applicable.

Familiarity informs the Difficulty alongside access, quality, and conditions.
These are provisional starting points, not automatic difficulties:

| Situation | Starting point |
|---|---|
| Familiar, accessible material with no meaningful risk | No roll; gather it with sufficient time. |
| Familiar material with a meaningful complication | Difficulty **10** |
| Unfamiliar material that can reasonably be assessed and harvested | Difficulty **15** |
| Especially strange or demanding material | Difficulty **20** |

On success, obtain **one useful batch**: enough suitable material for one
small mundane invention or one meaningful contribution to a larger project.
Record its name and useful properties in inventory. This is an ordinary
quantity, not a second spendable combat currency.

On success with Fear, the batch is still obtained; the complication can concern
time, noise, effort, access, or the surroundings. On failure, suitable material
or sufficient quality is not obtained, and the normal roll consequences apply.
There is no additional automatic malfunction roll.

Retrying requires a changed approach, source, tools, or a meaningful investment
of time. Repeating the same roll in an unchanged situation does not create an
unlimited harvest.

### Make a mundane invention

With suitable materials and a few minutes of workable conditions, assemble
a **small mundane item with one clear practical function**. A successful
assessment or gathering roll already established the batch's suitability; a
straightforward assembly does not need a second roll for the same uncertainty.

Examples include a splint, hooked pole, collapsible sled, pulley arrangement,
climbing aid, or a light folding screen. A batch is incorporated into the item.
If the design also needs rope, a hinge, an anchor, or another specific component,
that component must actually be available. Borrowed PC gear needs consent.

In a dangerous scene, you may assemble a small improvised tool as part of the
action roll that puts it to use. Describe an achievable construction and spend
at least one Part through **Improvise** on that roll. It both supports the rapid
assembly and adds the die's value to the attempt. A Prepared Defense instead
uses its own deployment procedure and Part cost.

The item grants its ordinary fictional function. A new automatic heal,
permanent numerical bonus, guaranteed combat condition, superior weapon
profile, or domain-card power needs a separately agreed rule; the word
"mundane" does not supply one. A mundane trap can still be useful through
normal positioning, appropriate rolls, and consequences.

Larger, intricate, or lasting constructions use **Work on a Project** and a
GM-set progress countdown. Countdowns reflect the work and suitable resources,
not a universal recipe price. A small field invention does not build a
settlement building or grant a seasonal resource payout.

### Learn the material

After successfully building and testing something with an unfamiliar material,
agree one useful discovery with the GM. Add that material to your familiar
list, with a short note such as "bends well, but does not carry a heavy load."
You may also learn through a genuine Work on a Project study of a sample.

Merely picking up a sample does not establish mastery. Once learned, the
material behaves dependably within its tested uses. Learning does not make
an unsuitable material suitable for every invention.

## Subclass: Rigger

Choose the Rigger to travel on your own devices and bring companions through
danger. This is the proposed path for Redneck. Its **Spellcast trait is your
chosen Inventor trait**.

### Foundation — available at level 1

**Mechanical Wings.** You can fly using your personal travel rig. While
flying, you can mark **one Stress** to pick up and carry a willing creature
approximately your size or smaller. Ordinary flight does not cost Stress or
Prepared Parts.

**Long Strides.** While using your ground travel rig, you may mark **one
Stress** to move up to **Far range**, rather than Close, as part of an action
roll. The route must suit the device. This movement does not add to other
features that extend the same move; use the greatest permitted distance.

### Specialization — first available at level 5

**Rescue Harness.** For the same one-Stress cost, Mechanical Wings can carry
**two willing creatures**, each approximately your size or smaller, instead
of one.

**Aerial Impact.** While flying, spend **one Hope** after a successful attack
to add **1d8** to its damage. You can use this once per attack.

### Mastery — first available at level 8, after Specialization

**Flock Flight.** Once per long rest, spend **two Prepared Parts** to provide
temporary flying rigs to up to **your Proficiency** willing allies within
**Close range**. They can fly for the current scene or **ten minutes**,
whichever ends first. They control their own movement. When the duration ends,
the rigs allow only a controlled descent toward the ground beneath each user;
hazards at the landing point still matter. This grants flight,
not your carrying ability, Long Strides, or Aerial Impact.

**Perfected Impact.** Aerial Impact adds **1d12** instead of 1d8; its Hope
cost remains the same.

The short-duration rigs are borrowed equipment. Spent Parts remain spent when
they are returned. Personal wings remain reusable. Deliberate destruction or
loss of a rig can matter in the fiction; routine use with familiar materials
does not invite arbitrary breakdowns. Replace or repair damaged mundane rigs
through Scavenge and Build or a suitable project.

## Subclass: Fieldwright

Choose the Fieldwright to establish useful positions, make shelter for
companions, and carry a workshop into the wilderness. Its **Spellcast trait
is your chosen Inventor trait**.

### Foundation — available at level 1

**Shared Shelter.** Your Prepared Defenses accommodate **two creatures**
approximately your size or smaller, instead of one. They must stand together
behind the same defense, within Very Close range of each other.

**Braced Position.** While within **Melee range** of your Prepared Defense
and on its protected side, gain **+1 Evasion**. This bonus does not stack
across multiple defenses.

### Specialization — first available at level 5

**Reinforce.** When deploying a Prepared Defense, you may spend **one
additional Part** to reinforce it. The first adversary spotlight devoted to
breaking or dismantling it removes the reinforcement; a second such spotlight
can destroy it. This does not stop an enemy from moving around it.

**Field Workshop.** Once per long rest, when you take Work on a Project to
build or repair a mundane invention, you and one willing ally helping you
may each clear **one marked Armor Slot**.

### Mastery — first available at level 8, after Specialization

**Shelter for All.** Your Prepared Defenses can accommodate up to **your
Proficiency plus one creatures**, instead of two. They must form one group
within Very Close range of the defense and remain on its protected side.
The normal one- or two-Part defense costs remain unchanged.

**Load-bearing.** While within Melee range of your Prepared Defense and on
its protected side, increase your **Severe damage threshold by 4**. This
benefit does not stack across multiple defenses and ends when you leave the
position or the defense stops functioning.

## Advancement through level 10

Use normal Daggerheart advancements and domain acquisition. These subclass
cards are choices; reaching their earliest levels does not automatically
grant them. Take Foundation, then Specialization, then Mastery, respecting
normal advancement slots and multiclass choices.

| Level | Class progression |
|---|---|
| **1** | Chassis, chosen Inventor trait, Pull Together, Prepared Parts, Scavenge and Build, one subclass Foundation, and two level-1 domain cards. |
| **2** | Standard tier achievements and advancement choices; choose a domain card of level 2 or lower. A trait increase may enlarge the next session's reserve. |
| **3** | Standard advancements; choose a domain card of level 3 or lower. |
| **4** | Standard advancements; choose a domain card of level 4 or lower. |
| **5** | Standard tier achievements and advancements; Specialization becomes an available choice; choose a domain card of level 5 or lower. |
| **6** | Standard advancements; Specialization remains selectable if not taken; choose a domain card of level 6 or lower. |
| **7** | Standard advancements; choose a domain card of level 7 or lower. |
| **8** | Standard tier achievements and advancements; Mastery becomes an available choice after Specialization; choose a domain card of level 8 or lower. |
| **9** | Standard advancements; choose a domain card of level 9 or lower. |
| **10** | Standard advancements; choose a domain card of level 10 or lower. |

No new domain-card library is required. Amme may retain Mending Touch and
Forceful Push at level 1. Additional cards remain personal choices, with
their printed loadout, recall, and use limits.

## Background questions

1. Who taught you a material lesson you have never forgotten, and what happened when you ignored it?
2. What invention do you hope to bring home, and whose respect do you want to earn?
3. Who was left behind during your upbringing, and how do you feel about that decision now?

## Connection questions

1. Which piece of your equipment have you trusted me to alter, and what did I promise you?
2. When did I get us out of danger with an invention you doubted?
3. What do you keep carrying that I insist could become something useful?

These are invitations, not required answers. Redneck's clan practices do not
force him to abandon companions or adopt one particular moral position.

## Three play examples

### A charge across open ground

Redneck has two Parts showing **2** and **4**. He spends the 2 to deploy a
folding screen within Close range. Its low face does not weaken it. Kaja
stands behind it; arrows through that screen have disadvantage. A swordsman
who reaches its unprotected side can attack her normally.

Alternatively, Redneck could spend both Parts for blocked line of sight.
Neither he nor Kaja could shoot directly through that barricade. He must
choose a position before the enemy's attack is resolved.

If Amme instead wants the stilts-and-hair lasso, Kaja's player agrees to the
use of her hair. Redneck makes an appropriate roll for rapidly rigging and
using it, spending the 4 through Improvise. The ordinary roll decides whether
it catches, trips, or redirects the intended target; it does not automatically
restrain an entire charging group.

### Crossing and rescue

A Rigger starts Far from someone who needs help. Redneck marks one Stress
for Long Strides and moves up to Far as part of his action roll, if the ground
and stilts permit it. Reaching Melee range is possible only if that destination
actually lies within the permitted move; "Far" is a distance band, not teleportation.

He can then use his flying rig and, on an appropriate later opportunity, mark
one Stress to carry a willing companion. Pull Together can instead move him
and one nearby willing ally at level 1 through a plausible rigged route for
three Hope. These are distinct costs and opportunities, not one free sequence
of every movement feature.

### An unfamiliar material

Amme wants a collapsible splint from a new kind of wood. Oscar judges that
assessing and obtaining a suitable batch has Difficulty **15**. Redneck's
roll totals **14**. He spends a Part showing **3** through Improvise, giving
a total of **17**. The original Hope/Fear outcome remains unchanged.

He gets the batch. With a few minutes and available bindings, the simple
splint needs no second roll for the same material assessment. After a useful
test, Amme and Oscar record the learned property. This material is now familiar
for that kind of use. The splint supports ordinary treatment; it does not
produce a free Mending Touch effect.

## Rules review and playtest cases

The wording was reviewed against these situations. Expected outcomes are
written rules consequences, not live-play results.

| Case | Expected handling |
|---|---|
| Parts show 1 and 4 | Either die buys cover. Both buy blocked sight. Choosing the lower die preserves the better roll/Hope option. |
| No Parts remain | Ordinary fabrication, attacks, domain cards, flight, and other resources still work. Gathering does not refill Parts. |
| No Hope remains and Stress is full | No Pull Together or Stress-powered movement; ordinary Rigger flight, normal movement, attacks, and safe crafting remain. |
| A ranged enemy changes angle | The directional barrier may no longer obstruct its attack. |
| An enemy attacks an exposed ally | Another nearby ally's cover does not automatically protect them. |
| A full barricade stands between both sides | Both sides lack sight through it. It is not a one-way shooting window. |
| An effect does not require that line of sight | Resolve the effect normally; the barricade grants no universal immunity. |
| Two defenses overlap | Cover does not multiply disadvantage; the Fieldwright's personal bonuses do not stack with themselves. |
| An enemy breaks reinforced cover | One suitable destruction spotlight removes reinforcement; another can break the defense. Repositioning remains possible. |
| A borrowed rope is packed away | Recover the rope when plausible; never refund the spent Parts. |
| A new material is gathered repeatedly | Collect actual available batches; no fresh Parts, Hope, or automatic settlement resources are produced. |
| A player proposes a new armor or weapon | Use an agreed equipment profile and normal tier restrictions; a batch is not permission for an unlimited statistical upgrade. |
| A device assists a social scene | Describe a plausible demonstration, repair, or practical aid. Ordinary Presence rolls and Improvise can apply. |
| A low-ceiling fight prevents useful flight | Ground movement, defenses, ordinary attacks, and Valor/Splendor choices remain available. |
| Another feature also extends movement | Long Strides uses the greatest permitted distance, not a sum of range bands. |

### Highest-priority balance checks

1. **Defense value over a whole scene.** Track whether one or two Parts routinely prevent most enemy contributions. Test open ground, several firing angles, melee pressure, and a narrow corridor. Size, direction, duration, and repositioning are the first adjustment points.
2. **Reserve frequency.** With a trait of +2, Redneck has two Parts per session. Test whether that is an interesting choice or whether it makes the central feature too scarce. Do not add unlimited replenishment to solve this.
3. **Rigger mobility.** Compare level-1 wings/carrying/stilts with Winged Sentinel. Long Strides is the new addition; aerial damage is deliberately delayed to Specialization.
4. **Fieldwright usefulness.** Compare shared cover and positioning bonuses against Rigger flight in actual campaign terrain, including scenes without ranged enemies.
5. **High-level group powers.** Test the short group flight and group barricade separately before using them in a serious level-8 scene.
6. **Fabrication pace.** Check whether simple devices support adventure or produce lengthy shopping and engineering discussions. Keep one material note and one item function readable at the table.

The main unresolved design choices are the session-based reserve, whether
Splendor remains the best long-term domain fit, and the defense's practical
footprint. These have complete provisional rules above, so the class can be
tested without first settling every taste question.
